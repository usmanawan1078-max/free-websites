How to Stop Spam Calls on Android and iPhone

You can't stop spam calls completely — spammers rotate numbers constantly — but you can cut them down dramatically with tools already on your phone. No paid app required.

The golden rule: don't engage

If you don't recognise a number, let it go to voicemail. Answering tells the caller's system your number is live, which usually means more calls, not fewer. Pressing a number to "opt out" or arguing with the caller has the same effect. A genuine caller will leave a message or text you.

On iPhone: silence unknown callers

  1. Open Settings, then Phone.
  2. Turn on Silence Unknown Callers.
  3. Calls from numbers not in your contacts, recent calls, or messages will go straight to voicemail and appear in your recent calls list — so you can still check them and call back if one turns out to be real.

One trade-off to know: a doctor's office, delivery driver, or school calling from a new number will also be silenced. If you're expecting such a call, add the number to your contacts first, or check voicemail promptly.

On Android: use the built-in spam protection

  1. Open the Phone app, tap the three-dot menu, then Settings.
  2. Look for Caller ID & spam (wording varies by brand) and turn on "See caller and spam ID" and "Filter spam calls".
  3. Suspected spam calls will be flagged on screen, and filtered ones won't ring at all.

On Samsung phones, the same options live in the Phone app under Settings > Caller ID and spam protection. On Pixel phones, they are in the Phone app under Settings > Spam and Call Screen, which can even answer and screen unknown calls for you.

Block numbers one by one

  1. Open your recent calls list and tap the number.
  2. Choose Block (and on most phones, Report spam at the same time).
  3. Repeat for repeat offenders. Blocking is free, instant, and reversible.

Blocking works best against persistent numbers. Against number-rotating robocallers, the silence/filter settings above matter more than blocking each call individually.

Register with a do-not-call list

Most countries run an official do-not-call registry (for example, the US has the National Do Not Call Registry; the UK has the Telephone Preference Service). Registration is free and reduces legitimate marketing calls, though it won't stop scammers who ignore the law. Search for your country's official registry — use the government site, not a lookalike site asking for payment.

What not to do

  • Don't call back unknown numbers, especially international ones you don't recognise — some are premium-rate traps.
  • Don't install a paid "call blocker" app before trying the built-in tools — most of what they do, your phone already does for free.
  • Never share codes, passwords, or bank details with any caller, even one who sounds official. Real banks and agencies don't ask for those by phone. If in doubt, hang up and call the organisation on its official number.

If spam calls suddenly explode

A sudden surge sometimes follows your number being shared or leaked. Tighten the settings above, report the worst numbers, and give it a week — surges usually fade. If calls include threats or harassment, keep a record and report them to your carrier and local authorities.
