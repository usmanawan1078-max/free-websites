How to Free Up Storage on an Android Phone

Running out of space is usually caused by a few big things: videos, old downloads, and apps you stopped using months ago. Work through these steps in order, and check your storage after each one — you may not need to do them all.

1. See what is actually using the space

  1. Open Settings, then tap Storage. (On some phones it sits under "Device care" or "About phone" — the name varies by brand, but every Android phone has a storage screen.)
  2. Look at the breakdown: Apps, Photos and videos, Audio, and System usually take the most room. This tells you where to start instead of deleting things at random.
  3. Many phones also offer a "Free up space" or "Clean up" button on this screen. It suggests safe deletions, like blurry duplicate photos or old screenshots. Review the list before confirming — never accept a cleanup blindly.

2. Clear app caches (safe, and often surprisingly effective)

  1. Go to Settings > Apps and tap an app that uses a lot of storage — browsers, social apps, and video apps are the usual suspects.
  2. Tap Storage & cache, then Clear cache.
  3. Repeat for the biggest apps. Your logins, photos, and chats stay untouched.
Don't confuse "Clear cache" with "Clear storage." Clearing storage resets the app: it can sign you out and delete downloaded content inside that app. Only use it if you are prepared to set the app up again.

3. Remove apps you no longer use

On the storage screen, sort apps by size. Uninstall games and apps you haven't opened in months. If you're unsure about an app, leave it — uninstalling a system app you actually need is harder to undo than living with it a little longer. You can always reinstall a free app later from the Play Store.

4. Deal with photos and videos (the biggest win)

  1. Open your gallery and delete burst shots, accidental recordings, and duplicate photos first — these add up silently.
  2. If you use Google Photos, open it and use its "Free up space" option. This removes copies already backed up from your phone while keeping them in your Google account. Only do this after confirming backup is complete — the app tells you when it is.
  3. For videos, consider moving large ones to a computer or cloud drive instead of keeping every clip on the phone. See our guide on transferring photos from phone to laptop.

5. Clean out Downloads and old files

Open the Files app and check the Downloads folder. Old PDFs, installers, memes, and forwarded documents collect there for years. Delete what you don't need, and move anything important to a cloud folder first.

6. What not to do

  • Don't install random "phone cleaner" or "RAM booster" apps. Most do little, show aggressive ads, and some collect more data than they clean. Your phone's built-in tools are enough.
  • Don't delete files from system folders you don't recognise. If you don't know what a file is, leave it alone.
  • Don't factory-reset the phone just to free space. That erases everything and is never the right fix for a full-storage warning.

When is it time for a bigger fix?

If you have done all of the above and still have almost no space, your phone may simply have a small storage capacity for the way you use it now. Options: use a cloud photo service, move media to a computer regularly, or, when you next buy a phone, choose one with more storage. A microSD card helps only if your phone has a card slot — check before buying one.
