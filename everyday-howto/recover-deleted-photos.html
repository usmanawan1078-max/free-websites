How to Recover Deleted Photos on Android and iPhone

First, the honest truth: most deleted photos are recoverable — but only if you act quickly and only through the trash folder or a backup you already had. No app, trick, or website can guarantee recovery. Here is what to try, in the order that works best.

Step 0: Stop using the phone heavily right now

When a photo is deleted, the phone marks its space as reusable. New photos, downloads, and app updates can overwrite it, making recovery impossible. So before anything else: stop taking new photos and pause big downloads until you have checked the trash folder. This is the single most useful thing you can do in the first minutes.

On iPhone: check Recently Deleted

  1. Open Photos, scroll down, and tap Recently Deleted. (You may need to unlock it with your passcode.)
  2. Deleted photos and videos stay here for 30 days. Select what you want, tap Recover, and it returns to your library.
  3. If the album is empty, the 30 days have passed or the photo was deleted from this album already — move on to the backup checks below.

On Android: check Trash

  1. Open Google Photos (or your phone brand's gallery app — Samsung Gallery, for example, has its own trash).
  2. Tap Library, then Trash (in some versions: Bin).
  3. Deleted items usually stay for 30 days. Select them and tap Restore.
  4. If you use a brand gallery app, check its trash too — deleting from one app does not always empty the other app's trash.

Check your cloud backups

  1. Google Photos: open the app and search your library — if backup was on, the photo may still be in the cloud even if it left the phone. Check the Google Photos website from a computer too.
  2. iCloud: on an iPhone, check Settings > your name > iCloud > Photos to see whether sync was on, and check the iCloud website from a computer.
  3. Other backups: check WhatsApp chat backups, shared albums, and any messaging app where you may have sent the photo to someone — a copy sent to a friend or family member is often the easiest recovery of all.

What about photo recovery apps?

Be very careful here. Many recovery apps do little more than show you the same trash folder you can check yourself, while showing ads or asking for broad permissions. Some are outright scams. A legitimate app cannot recover photos that have been permanently deleted and overwritten — that is a technical limit, not a feature locked behind payment. Never pay an app that promises guaranteed recovery, and never grant a recovery app access to your accounts or payment details.

If the photo is not in trash and not in any backup, paid data-recovery services exist, but they are expensive, they require handing over your device, and success is far from certain. For most personal photos, that cost is hard to justify — but the option exists if the photo is truly irreplaceable.

What does not work

  • Restarting the phone repeatedly — this does not restore anything and risks overwriting data.
  • Websites that claim to recover deleted photos online by entering your phone number — these are scams. Do not enter your number or any code they send you.
  • Factory-resetting the phone — this destroys far more than it could ever recover.

Prevent the next loss: turn on backup now

The real fix is a backup that runs before you need it. See our guide on backing up your phone for free — it takes a few minutes, costs nothing, and turns the next accident into a minor annoyance instead of a disaster.
