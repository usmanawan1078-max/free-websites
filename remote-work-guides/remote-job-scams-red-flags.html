Remote job scams: red flags every beginner must know

Beginners are the prime target for job scams, because scammers know you are eager and unfamiliar with normal hiring. Learn the patterns once and you will spot most of them instantly.

Advertisement

Red flag 1: You have to pay to get hired

Training fees, equipment deposits, background-check payments, "account activation" charges, software you must buy from their specific seller — all versions of the same trick. Real employers do not charge you to start work. The moment money flows from you to the "employer" before your first paycheque, stop. This rule alone filters out most scams.

Red flag 2: The fake cheque

A "company" sends you a cheque for equipment or supplies and asks you to deposit it, keep a portion, and forward the rest. The cheque later bounces, but your forwarded money is already gone. No legitimate employer pays this way. The same trick appears with wire transfers and gift cards: any job that involves moving money for someone else is not a job, it is money laundering with you as the cut-out.

Red flag 3: Reshipping and "package handling" jobs

Offers to receive packages at home and forward them, often framed as logistics or quality inspection. These are usually fronts for moving fraudulently purchased goods. Your address becomes part of a crime, and you are the one the police can find.

Red flag 4: No real company behind the offer

Warning signs: the interview happens only over chat, nobody will do a video call, the company website is days old or copies another firm's text, the email address is a free mailbox instead of a company domain, and the job post promises unusually high pay for simple work. Before accepting anything, search the company name plus the word "scam," check its website's age and contact details, and verify the recruiter on professional networks. A real company leaves traces; a scam leaves only the ad.

Red flag 5: Stolen job posts and impersonation

Scammers copy real job listings from real companies and repost them with their own contact details. If a recruiter contacts you about a role, go to the company's own careers page and check whether the role exists, and contact the company through its official channels — never through the number the recruiter gave you.

Red flag 6: Pressure and secrecy

"Reply within two hours or the offer goes to someone else." "Don't tell anyone about this opportunity yet." Pressure and secrecy are control tactics. Real hiring processes tolerate questions and a day to think. Slow down whenever someone rushes you.

What to do if you spot one

  • Stop replying and do not send money, documents, or bank details.
  • Save screenshots of the post, messages, and any payment requests.
  • Report the listing to the platform where you found it.
  • If you already paid, contact your bank or payment provider immediately and report it to your local fraud reporting service.
  • Never share identity documents, bank logins, or one-time codes with a recruiter — no real employer asks for these over chat.
The one-line test: a real job pays you. If the money flows from you to them first, it is not a job.
