DAAD Scholarships for Pakistani Students: EPOS, Eligibility, Deadlines

Last updated: October 2026. Dates below name their cycle. Verify every date on daad.de and on your chosen course page before you apply.

DAAD — the German Academic Exchange Service — is not one scholarship. It is a family of programmes, and the first mistake Pakistani applicants make is searching for "the DAAD deadline" as if one existed. There is no single date. Each programme, and under EPOS each course, sets its own deadline. Pakistan is one of DAAD's major partner countries, and Pakistani graduates have won DAAD funding for decades, but you have to match yourself to the right programme first.

The programmes that matter most for Pakistanis

  • EPOS — Development-Related Postgraduate Courses: the main route. It funds master's degrees (and some PhDs) at selected German universities in fields connected to development: renewable energy, public health, water and environmental management, agriculture, urban planning, economics, engineering and related areas.
  • Helmut-Schmidt Programme: master's degrees in public policy and good governance for future leaders from developing countries.
  • Research grants: for doctoral candidates and young academics planning a PhD or research stay in Germany. Rules and deadlines differ from EPOS, so read the programme page, not a summary.

EPOS eligibility: the four tests

  1. Degree: a four-year bachelor's degree (usually 16 years of education) in a subject related to the course, with results roughly in the upper third of your class.
  2. Degree age: your bachelor's degree should normally be no more than six years old at the time of application.
  3. Work experience: at least two years of relevant professional experience gained after your bachelor's degree. Internships and voluntary work generally do not count toward this requirement — this is stricter than Chevening's hours rule, so read it carefully.
  4. Language: English proof such as IELTS or TOEFL for English-taught courses (institutional TOEFL is generally not accepted), or German at B1 level or above for German-taught courses. For English-taught courses, a Medium of Instruction certificate from your university is accepted instead of IELTS at most programmes — confirm it on your chosen course page.

You also need a clear, development-related motivation and a believable plan to contribute back in Pakistan. EPOS is a development programme; a motivation letter that reads like a generic study-abroad essay will not survive selection.

Deadlines: per course, not per programme

Under EPOS you do not apply to DAAD directly. You choose courses from the official EPOS list and apply to each university course directly, following that course's own deadline. For courses starting in the 2027/28 academic year, most application windows fell between August and November 2026, with some closing as early as late August and others running into November.

One published example from this cycle: the MSc in Environmental Governance at the University of Freiburg, starting October 2027, opened 15 June 2026 and closed 15 October 2026, with a monthly stipend of around €992 for master's students under the 2026 rates. Treat stipend figures as cycle-specific and verify the current rate on the course page.

Because deadlines are per course, your timeline is: shortlist courses from the EPOS list first, note each course's own date, and work backwards from the earliest one.

The APS certificate: start it early

Pakistani applicants to German universities generally need an APS certificate, issued through aps-pakistan.de, before their applications are complete. It verifies your academic documents and takes weeks to process, so apply for it well before your earliest course deadline — leaving it to the final month is the most common avoidable reason for a missed DAAD cycle.

Documents you will usually need

  • The DAAD application form and the course's own application form, where the course asks for both.
  • A motivation letter tied to development relevance, not just personal ambition.
  • A CV, degree certificate and full academic transcript.
  • Language proof (IELTS/TOEFL, Medium of Instruction letter, or German certificate as applicable).
  • Reference letters and, where asked, proof of employment covering your two years of experience.

For Indian students

The same EPOS rules apply to Indian applicants: degree age, two years of relevant post-degree experience, upper-third record, and per-course deadlines through the EPOS list. DAAD runs a regional office in New Delhi (daad.in), which publishes India-specific information sessions and programme lists — start there rather than from a blog summary. Indian applicants to German universities should also check whether their course requires an APS certificate through aps-india.de, and confirm that requirement on the course page itself, since it varies by institution. There is no India-only EPOS deadline; your deadline is your chosen course's deadline.
